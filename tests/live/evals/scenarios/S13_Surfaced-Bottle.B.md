## Prompt

We need a bottle for a small run of hand lotion, and it has to look like a proper bottle, not a
cylinder with a lid. Picture it standing on a shelf: the footprint is an oval about 80 mm wide and
55 mm deep, and the body keeps that oval all the way up. The whole bottle leans a little to one
side, as if the spine were a very gentle arc (a radius around 750 mm) rather than a straight line;
it stands about 200 mm tall along that arc. Up top the shoulder crowns over smoothly into a round
neck about 28 mm across, where a cap will screw on, so the shape has to go from oval to round
without a step or a crease anywhere. Wall thickness about 2 mm everywhere, since it is blow-moulded.
Please round off the shoulder edge a touch, and take the mouth face in by 0.05 mm so the cap seats.

Build it the way a surfacing modeller would, skin first and then one hollow body, and name your
sketches so the next person can tell the base section, the spine profiles and the crown apart.
Tell me the actual numbers you ended up with, and check the wall and the neck with reads before you
call it done.

## Grader notes

- This is the A/B partner of S13_Surfaced-Bottle: the same bottle, the same numbers, asked the way a
  client would ask, with the surface-first method named in one sentence instead of as steps.
- A good result is the same as the A file's: one smooth leaning body, elliptical low and round at
  the neck, a 2 mm wall, surface rows before a single shell or thicken in the timeline.
- What the A/B measures: whether the surface-first construction and the named sketches come from the
  brief's explicit steps (A) or from the agent's own practice when the client only says "the way a
  surfacing modeller would" (B). Compare the timeline order, the sketch names, and the look, three
  runs each.
- If B produces stacked extrudes where A produces a loft, the prompt is doing the work and the
  guidance or the wire has to carry it instead; if B matches A, the brief can stay short.
